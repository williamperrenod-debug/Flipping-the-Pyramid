Developing Critical Thinking Skills with GenAI

Generative AI can support learning across levels of Bloom's Revised Taxonomy by encouraging questioning, analysis, reflection, and creation. This interactive companion explores three AI-supported assignments designed to develop critical thinking skills.

Explore Paired Critical Thinking Skills

Each example demonstrates how GenAI can support student learning while maintaining student agency, reflection, and disciplinary thinking.

Colloquial Language, Standard Language, or Educated Language?

Why this pairing

Students generate language with ChatGPT while critically evaluating AI-generated responses. They compare regional dialects, analyze linguistic variation, revise AI-generated language into academic writing, and reflect on the strengths, limitations, and ethical use of AI.

Learning Focus

  • Creating original language
  • Evaluating AI-generated output
  • Revising for audience and purpose
  • Reflecting on responsible AI use
  • Developing rhetorical awareness

Faculty Reflection

How can students learn to critique AI-generated work before incorporating it into their own writing?

Designing a Research Study: Controlling for a Variable and Using ChatGPT

Why this pairing

Students apply research methods while analyzing variables, research design, and AI-generated suggestions. ChatGPT supports brainstorming and revision, but students remain responsible for evaluating evidence and designing sound research methods.

Learning Focus

  • Analyze research questions
  • Apply research methods
  • Design investigations
  • Evaluate AI suggestions
  • Strengthen analytical reasoning

Faculty Reflection

Where should AI support student thinking, and where should students rely on their own analytical reasoning?

Oral Presentation on Your Research Project

Why this pairing

Students synthesize a semester-long research project into a concise three-minute presentation supported by AI-generated flashcards created with Revisely. The assignment reinforces understanding by requiring students to summarize research, explain evidence, and reflect on their research process.

Learning Focus

  • Remember key details from research
  • Demonstrate understanding of research questions
  • Summarize main arguments
  • Explain how evidence supports claims
  • Reflect on research and writing processes

Faculty Reflection

How does transforming written research into a brief oral presentation deepen student understanding and long-term retention?
